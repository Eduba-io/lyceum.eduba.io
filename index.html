The Lyceum

I spent 8 years in the Marine Corps working on cryptographic systems and F-35/F-18 avionics. Zero-defect environments. When something breaks at 30,000 feet, you don't get to iterate.

I started seeing patterns. The same patterns that made fighter jets work made AI systems work. The same abstraction layers. The same question: what layer does this problem belong on?

“The people who built refrigeration technology weren't the ones who got rich. Coca-Cola did. The question was never about the technology. It was about what the technology made possible.”

I went to Edinburgh to study AI governance. I started teaching faculty at a small college. Then Pacific Life called. Then KPMG. Then IAG. Then the research got published.

Somewhere in there, I built a system that took 160 hours of manual compliance work and made it take 5 minutes. I trained 300+ employees across Fortune 500 companies. 95% were still using what I taught them 30 days later.

I've never offered a certification before. Eduba has trained thousands, but we've never put our name on a credential. This is the first time. And I want to make it count.

Three Truths
01

They say:

“AI is moving too fast. I can't keep up.”

The Industry Is Predictable

The transformer paper came out in 2017. Almost a decade ago. That's not fast. That's a pattern playing out. Jacquard looms predicted Turing. Turing predicted Unix. Unix predicted this. If you learned to see patterns in 2020, you'd have predicted everything since.

If I understand the pattern, I can predict what's next.

02

They say:

“I'm not technical enough for this.”

Concepts Transfer Across Roles

The methodology is the same. What changes is the application. A developer applies folder architecture to code. A business owner applies it to operations. A creator applies it to content. The thinking is identical.

There's a version of this for me.

03

They say:

“Another AI course will be outdated in 6 months.”

We Teach Thinking, Not Tools

If Claude disappeared tomorrow, what would you still know? Most courses: nothing. This one: everything. Because we teach the layer beneath the tools. Symbols capture processes. Edge cases need precision; knowledge cases need understanding. This was true in 1950. It'll be true in 2035.

This is an investment in how I think, not what I use.
